# Phase 1 — Business Understanding
## 02. Business Problem

---

## 1. The Problem

Modern retailers operate across multiple channels — mobile apps, websites, and third-party marketplaces — and generate data at every step of the customer journey: browsing, cart activity, purchases, reviews, and marketing exposure. In most organizations, that data sits in disconnected systems: a transactional database that knows what was bought, a marketing platform that knows what was clicked, an app analytics tool that knows what was browsed — and no single place that connects the three. The result is that retail leadership ends up making growth decisions on partial information, or on intuition dressed up as data.

ORGEE was built to close that gap: to connect transaction data, behavioral data, and marketing data into one coherent model, and use it to answer the specific strategic questions a retail leadership team actually needs answered.

## 2. The Questions This Project Answers

- **Where do customers abandon their journey, and where is that abandonment worst?** Not a general sense of "conversion could be better," but a specific, ranked breakdown of funnel stages.
- **Which customers are most valuable, and by how much?** Not "our best customers," but a quantified segmentation with dollar figures attached to each group.
- **Does personalizing product recommendations actually improve outcomes?** Not an assumption that personalization helps because it's an industry norm, but a tested answer, backed by a controlled experiment with a predetermined decision framework.
- **Do marketing channels perform differently enough to justify reallocating budget?** Not a comparison of raw numbers that could be explained by chance, but a statistically grounded answer.
- **What would revenue look like under a hypothetical improvement, and which lever is worth pursuing first?** Not a single static report, but an interactive tool a decision-maker can actually manipulate.

## 3. Why This Matters

A retailer that can't answer these questions with real data ends up in one of two failure modes: either paralysis (no decision gets made because no one is confident in the numbers), or overconfidence (a decision gets made based on an assumption that was never actually tested — like assuming personalized recommendations help, without ever checking). Both are expensive. The cost of the first is missed opportunity; the cost of the second is wasted investment in a change that doesn't move the metric it was meant to move. ORGEE's recommendation engine and marketing experiment findings are direct, concrete examples of this second failure mode being caught *before* it became a wasted investment, rather than after.

## 4. The Business Question

> **If management changes a key business lever, what could happen to revenue, customers, conversion, and customer value — and is the current recommendation and marketing strategy actually working?**

This single question threads through all ten phases of ORGEE:

- Phases 2–3 build the data foundation accurate enough to answer it with confidence.
- Phases 4–6 establish what is currently true about revenue, the customer journey, and customer value.
- Phases 7–8 test a specific, concrete growth hypothesis (personalized recommendations) with real rigor, rather than leaving it as an assumption.
- Phase 9 puts the current state of the business in front of decision-makers.
- Phase 10 lets those decision-makers test hypothetical changes against the real, verified baseline before acting on them.

## 5. Scope of the Problem

This project addresses the growth and product-analytics side of retail operations — customer behavior, product performance, personalization, and experimentation. It does not address supply-chain, logistics, fraud/risk (covered separately in this portfolio's Project 1), or financial/accounting operations. See `09_Project_Scope.md` for the complete boundary of what was and wasn't built.
